## H1: Metabolism-subtype gastric cancer cells may exhibit increased sensitivity to GPX4 inhibition or glutathione depletion, potentially revealing a subtype-specific ferroptosis vulnerability

- Increased abundance of proteins involved in the glutathione–GPX4 system in the Metabolism subtype
- This may indicate a greater dependency on ferroptosis defence mechanisms
- Inhibiting these protective mechanisms could increase sensitivity to ferroptosis and potentially represent a therapeutic strategy

## H2: MYC-target protein abundance is tightly coupled to the proteostasis (UPR) and DNA-repair machinery across the panel, independent of subtype, predicting a proteostasis vulnerability in high-MYC lines

- strong positive correlation of MYC-target protein abundance with UPR proteins
- moderate correlation with DNA repair proteins
- already known: MYC-driven cell growth and protein synthesis increase proteotoxic and replication stress and maintains UPR activation and DNA repair mechanisms to maintain cancer cell survival
- support on proteomic level and association between MYC activity, proteostasis and DNA repair across gastric cancer cell lines
- highly MYC-active cells may exhibit increased dependencies on both proteostasis and DNA repair mechanisms, potentially representing therapeutic vulnerabilities that warrant further investigation
